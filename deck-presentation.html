Section 1

The Problem

Asymmetrical reporting and fragmented data force entrant companies to navigate a complex, broker-driven setup chain.

The Players in the Long Chain

Global entrants deal with a fragmented web: commercial landlords, legal advisors, tax auditors, BOT operators, recruiters, EOR platforms, and state IT promotion agencies.

The Epistemic Disparity: Reports publish conflicting counts for the exact same city without underlying entity data. In Bengaluru, Vestian reports 487 GCCs (standalone parent leases), JLL reports 900+ units (physical business branches), while Flexiple lists ~870 entities.

Who is Hurt by the Asymmetry?

  • Global Entrant CXO (Hypothesis for Discovery): Lacks an uncorrupted single source of truth; spends months in feasibility cycles and risks misallocating micro-markets based on commercial broker sales pitches.
  • Legitimate Service Providers: Quality boutique BOT advisors lose bids to aggressive brand marketing rather than verified delivery outcomes.
  • What They Do Today: Rely almost entirely on informal referrals, word-of-mouth WhatsApp advice, and PDF consultant reports.

The Solution: Deterministic Location Engine

4-question criteria with 100% visible math. No black-box AI, no paid placement.

4 Strategic Input Parameters

  • 1. Industry Sector: Tech/SaaS, BFSI, Healthcare/Pharma, Auto/Manufacturing, Aerospace, Retail.
  • 2. Planned Team Size in 3 Years: <100 (Seed pod), 100-500 (Growth), 500-2,000, 2,000+.
  • 3. Centre Mandate: Core Software/AI, Finance & Risk, Hardware/R&D, Operations.
  • 4. Decisive Priority (40% Weight): Cost Arbitrage, Talent Depth, Speed to Hire, State Subsidies.

Transparent Mathematical Scoring Rule

Total Score = (Priority Metric × 4.0) + Sector Fit (25) + Function Fit (20) + Scale Factor (15) + Base Ecosystem (10)

Generates a ranked Top 3 shortlist with plain-language rank justifications, real peer GCC anchors, and honest CXO caveats (e.g., ORR traffic density, BKC rent premiums).
